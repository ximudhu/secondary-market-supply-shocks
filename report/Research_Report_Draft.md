## 1. Introduction

Secondary markets for limited-edition collectibles (e.g., designer toys, licensed character merchandise) are characterised by persistent scarcity premiums driven by supply-side closure enforced by original manufacturers. When brand owners execute *official resupply events* — unexpected or semi-announced re-releases of previously scarce SKUs — they introduce an exogenous supply shock into these otherwise closed ecosystems. This paper investigates the magnitude and dynamics of price collapse following such shocks, employing an event-study framework anchored on two distinct event dates.

**Research Question:** To what degree, and at what pace, does an official secondary-supply announcement and subsequent physical release erode the scarcity premium embedded in secondary-market transaction prices?

## 3. Data & Methodology

### 3.1 Data Sources

All transaction-level price data were sourced from a major Japanese peer-to-peer collectibles marketplace. Raw exports were organised per intellectual property (IP) in subdirectories under `data_raw/`, with meta-information (IP names, event dates) centralised in `config_meta.csv`.

### 3.2 Event Windows and Dual-Anchor Design

Two event dates are defined for each IP:

| Symbol | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **T1** | Official announcement date of the re-release |
| **T2** | Physical product availability / shipment date (**anchor: Day 0**) |

All analysis windows are expressed in *relative* days with T2 = 0. Depending on data availability per IP, the event window spans either **[−30, +120]** or **[−30, +90]** trading days.

### 3.3 Relative Timeline Alignment

To enable cross-IP comparison despite heterogeneous calendar dates, each observation is mapped to a **relative day index** \( \tau = \text{date} - T2 \). This alignment ensures that structural breaks are superimposable across IPs without calendar confounding.

### 3.4 IQR Outlier Removal

Within each IP-day cell, transaction prices undergo **IQR-based outlier filtering**: observations falling below \( Q_1 - 1.5 \times IQR \) or above \( Q_3 + 1.5 \times IQR \) are excluded. This guards against artificially inflated "trophy" listings and data-entry errors that would bias the estimated price trajectory.

### 3.5 Multi-modal LLM Validation for Bulk-sale Noise Reduction

Marketplace listings frequently bundle multiple units into a single sale, causing per-unit prices to appear artificially depressed. We address this by submitting listing thumbnail images to a Vision LLM (GPT-4o or equivalent), which classifies each listing as *single-unit* or *bundle* and, for bundles, extracts the implied unit count to compute a normalised per-unit price. This step is critical for preserving the integrity of the price series in the post-shock window, where resellers commonly liquidate inventory in bulk.

### 3.6 Price Normalisation

Following cleaning, daily median transaction prices are normalised to the **[0, 1]** interval using the pre-event baseline window (days −30 to −1) as the reference range. This facilitates direct comparison of shock magnitude across IPs of differing absolute price levels.
